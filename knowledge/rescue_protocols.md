# Rescue Rover Operational Protocols

## Thermal Hazard Approach

When temperature exceeds 40C (warm) or 60C (overheat):
- Stop immediately and do not approach the heat source directly
- Announce the temperature reading to the operator
- Look for alternate routes around the heat zone
- If a person is near the heat source, speak calmly: "I detect high heat in this area. I'm going to find a safer path to reach you"
- Check for secondary hazards: smoke, flame glow, structural weakening from heat
- Priority: report the hazard location and temperature, then continue search from a safe distance

## Victim Communication Protocol

When a voice, distress call, or person is detected:
- Stop moving and listen for 3-5 seconds to confirm the sound
- Speak in a calm, clear, reassuring voice: "I hear you. I'm a rescue rover. Help is on the way. Can you tell me your name and if you're injured?"
- Wait for a response. If the person speaks, acknowledge what they said before acting
- If the person is injured and cannot move: report their location and condition, do not attempt to move them
- If the person is responsive and can follow instructions: guide them toward you with clear directional instructions
- Never promise specific timelines. Say "help is on the way" not "help will arrive in 10 minutes"
- If the person is panicking: speak slowly, use short sentences, repeat key information

## Audio Distress Recognition

Sounds that indicate a person in distress:
- Repeated calls for "help" or "hello" — human voice, directional
- Weak or muffled voice — person may be trapped or injured
- Tapping or knocking — person may be trapped under rubble
- Crying or whimpering — injured person, approach carefully

Sounds that indicate environmental hazard:
- Cracking or creaking — structural instability, risk of collapse
- Hissing — possible gas leak, do not approach
- Rumbling or grinding — earth movement or structural shift
- Loud impact — possible secondary collapse

When distress is detected: mark the location on the map, speak to acknowledge, and approach slowly with frequent stops to listen again.

## Structural Hazard Indicators

Visual signs of structural danger:
- Cracks in walls or ceiling — avoid area, report as hazard
- Leaning or displaced walls — risk of collapse, maintain 2m distance
- Rubble piles — may be unstable, do not climb, check for voids where survivors could be
- Exposed rebar or sharp metal — hazard, report location
- Water pooling — possible flooding or pipe rupture, electrical hazard risk

When a structural hazard is identified:
- Mark the location on the map with a hazard marker
- Do not drive over rubble or unstable surfaces
- Announce the hazard type and location to the operator
- Look for alternate routes

## Search Pattern Guidelines

Efficient search patterns for area coverage:
- Lawn-mowing pattern: drive in parallel lines, turning at each boundary
- Spiral pattern: start from the center and spiral outward
- Perimeter-first: circle the area boundary, then sweep inward
- Sound-guided: when audio distress is detected, break pattern and approach the sound source

Priority order for search:
1. Areas where distress calls or voices were heard
2. Areas with heat signatures (possible survivors)
3. Unexplored areas closest to current position
4. Areas with partial obstruction (voids in rubble where people could be trapped)

Mark explored areas on the map. Do not re-search already-explored areas unless new information suggests a survivor there.

## Triage and Assessment Protocol

When a survivor is found:
1. Announce: "Survivor located. Position marked on map."
2. Assess from a safe distance: Is the person conscious? Moving? Speaking?
3. If conscious and speaking: ask name, injuries, mobility
4. If unconscious: report location immediately, do not attempt to move
5. If trapped: report the type of obstruction and estimated location
6. Log the finding with type, location, and condition
7. Continue searching for additional survivors after logging

Priority levels:
- Immediate: conscious, trapped, needs extraction
- Urgent: conscious, injured but mobile
- Stable: conscious, uninjured, can self-evacuate with guidance

## Hazardous Material Indicators

Signs of hazardous materials:
- Unusual odors (do not approach if gas-like or chemical smell detected)
- Discolored pools or residue on surfaces
- Containers with hazard labels (if visible in camera)
- Sparking or arcing from electrical equipment

If hazardous materials are suspected:
- Do not approach closer
- Report the location and what was observed
- Mark as hazard on the map
- Route around the area and warn the operator
